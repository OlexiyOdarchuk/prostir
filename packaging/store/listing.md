## English

**Title:** Prostir

**Short description**

Extra monitors for a Linux PC, around you in the headset. Real desktop outputs:
windows, panels and the mouse move across them. Needs the free Prostir app on a PC
with KDE Plasma 6 or Hyprland.

**Long description**

Prostir puts extra monitors for your Linux laptop or desktop (KDE Plasma or Hyprland)
around you in Quest. They are real outputs of the PC: drag windows onto them, maximize
them, keep a panel or workspaces there. The mouse moves from one monitor to the next where they meet in front of
you, sideways and up or down.

Add, remove and arrange monitors from the headset: 1080p, 1440p, 4K, ultrawide or
portrait, up to six. Start around you, on a wall or above your head for lying down,
flat or curved, then grab any monitor and put it where you like. Let one go almost
against another and it sticks edge to edge; the A button closes the seams in your own
arrangement. The PC arranges its desktop the way you see it, and the laptop's own
screen is part of the arrangement too.

Text stays sharp: the video goes straight into the headset's compositor layers instead
of a flat window, and the PC encodes it on the GPU.

A single window can leave the desktop too: detach the PC's active window and it floats
as a panel of its own, closer than the monitors.

- Profiles for work, cinema and lying down, switched in one press
- Detach up to three PC windows into floating panels; move the active window to the
  monitor in front of you; save a screenshot of that monitor on the PC
- Monitors can follow your head; hold an open palm to your face for a round menu on it,
  with the time, the date and the battery in the middle
- Dim all monitors at once for the evening
- Move one monitor or the whole arrangement at once
- Focus mode dims every monitor except the one with the mouse; the "Mouse?" button
  shows where the pointer is, with rings, and on Hyprland the mouse can follow your gaze
- PC sound in the headset; PC apps hear the headset microphone while they record, and a
  switch in the headset menu turns the microphone off
- Optional sound from the screen: it comes from the monitor whose window is playing it
- Touch the menu with a finger; resize a monitor with both hands
- A window onto your real keyboard through the passthrough cameras; your room as the
  background, dimmed if you like
- Video quality, encoder and codec set from the headset; a 72 Hz mode for longer battery
  life
- Optional PC notifications in the headset and rest reminders, off until you turn them on
- Over your local Wi-Fi network; encrypted, and paired with your PC once
- English and Ukrainian

Requirements: a PC with KDE Plasma 6 or Hyprland on Wayland on the same network, and the
free Prostir app for the PC from prostir.ishawyha.dev. The PC encodes on the GPU where
it can (tested on Intel; AMD should work; NVIDIA is experimental) and on the CPU
otherwise. Prostir is free software (GPL-3.0-or-later).

### Permissions (why the app asks for them)

- **Microphone (RECORD_AUDIO):** the headset microphone becomes a microphone on the
  user's own PC. It records only while a PC app is recording and the microphone switch
  in the headset menu (Quick, or Settings → Sound) is on, and the sound goes only to that PC,
  encrypted. The PC can ask for the microphone but cannot turn the switch on.
- **Hand tracking (HAND_TRACKING):** pointing, pinching and touching the menu; drawing
  the hands.
- **OpenXR (org.khronos.openxr.permission.OPENXR and OPENXR_SYSTEM):** running as an
  OpenXR app on the headset's own runtime.
- **Network (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE):** finding and talking
  to the user's PC on the local network.
- **Keep awake (WAKE_LOCK):** keeps the headset's Wi-Fi in its low-latency mode while
  the app runs, so frames from the PC are not held back by the radio's power saving.

Not a permission: passthrough is an optional feature (`com.oculus.feature.PASSTHROUGH`,
not required) for the room backgrounds (plain and dimmed) and the keyboard window. The
system composites the cameras; the app never receives camera images.

### Data use

- The headset app sends nothing to the developer or any third party. Everything goes
  between the headset and the user's own PC, encrypted. It collects no account data,
  no analytics and no crash reports.
- Platform features used: entitlement check only.
- Finding the PC: the headset listens for the PC's broadcast on UDP port 47831. When it
  hears none, it also tries TCP port 47830 on every address of its own local /24 subnet
  to find the PC there. It sends nothing else to those addresses.
- The PC app is not part of the store listing. Its official builds count users: once a
  day, when a headset connects, the PC sends GoatCounter one request with only the date
  in it (and `/install` the first time). No identifier, host name or settings. A
  notification on the first start explains it and how to turn it off (tray item "Count
  this PC among users", or `PROSTIR_NO_COUNT=1`); the first count is sent no earlier
  than the next day (UTC). See the privacy policy.
- Answer the Data Use form accordingly: the headset app collects and shares no user
  data. Microphone audio is processed only to send it to the user's own PC.

### Age rating (IARC) notes

No violence, sexual content, gambling, drugs or user-to-user communication. The app
shows the user's own PC screens; it has no store of its own, no ads and no purchases.
Note it as an app that displays content from the user's own computer.

## Notes for the reviewer

Text for the testing-notes field of the submission.

Prostir is a companion app: it shows extra monitors of the user's own Linux PC. To test
everything you need:

- a PC running Linux with KDE Plasma 6 (Wayland session) or Hyprland, on the same Wi-Fi
  network as the headset, with no client isolation between devices;
- the free Prostir app for the PC from https://prostir.ishawyha.dev (packages for Arch,
  Debian/Ubuntu and Fedora, a Nix flake, or a tarball with `install.sh`).

Steps:

1. On the PC, install and start Prostir. Its icon appears in the system tray. If a
   firewall runs on the PC, allow TCP 47830 and UDP 47831.
2. On the headset, open Prostir. Without a PC it shows the start page with these
   requirements and "Looking for PCs on the network".
3. When the headset finds exactly one PC, it connects to it by itself; with several,
   select the PC in the list. The headset shows "A new PC" with the PC's name: press
   "Pair".
4. The headset shows a 6-digit code. Type it into the dialog that opens on the PC.
5. One 2560×1440 monitor appears in front of you. Add more in the menu's Monitors tab.
   Point at one, hold the grip button and move it; press A to line them up again. Open the menu (☰ or X on the left controller) and try the
   tabs: Quick (the Work, Cinema and Lying down profiles, and tiles such as in front of
   me, focus, cinema, PC sound, microphone, detach window and screenshot), Monitors (a
   map of the monitors to pick one, make primary, remove, add, line up, layout, curve,
   follow my head, stick together, drag one or all, save as profile) and Settings with
   its parts Look (backgrounds, focus mode, keyboard window, rest reminders), Controls
   (hands, menu size, which hand has the palm menu, display rate), Sound (where PC
   sound plays, microphone, sound from the screen), Language, PC (video quality,
   encoder, codec, notifications, paired PCs) and Help. With hand tracking, hold an
   open left palm towards your face for a quarter of a second to see the round palm
   menu, and press it with the right index finger.
6. Put a window on a virtual monitor from the PC; take the headset off and on again
   (streaming pauses and resumes).

If you cannot set up such a PC, the trailer attached to this submission shows Prostir
in use with a real PC: monitors around the user, windows moving between them, the menu
and hands. A short clip is also at https://prostir.ishawyha.dev/demo.mp4.

personal, non-commercial use and forbids modification unless the developer allows it.

store version must not add such restrictions (GPLv3 section 10).
